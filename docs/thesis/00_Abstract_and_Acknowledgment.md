# ABSTRACT

The informal sector, particularly artisanal trades such as carpentry, plumbing, and electrical repair, constitutes the primary engine of employment in Ghana. Historically, this ecosystem has operated offline through localized networking, leading to severe matching inefficiencies in rapidly expanding urban areas. While the global gig economy has introduced digital intermediation, the transition for traditional blue-collar artisans is hindered by a profound "trust deficit." Consumers fear poor workmanship and prepayment scams, while artisans face the risk of non-payment. Existing classified platforms operating in Ghana (e.g., Jiji, Tonaton) function merely as open directories; they lack integrated escrow payment systems, rigorous identity verification, and structured dispute resolution mechanisms, thereby shifting the entire transaction risk onto the users.

This study aims to design, develop, and evaluate "ArtisanConnect," a robust, AI-powered web-based escrow and discovery platform tailored specifically for the Ghanaian informal artisanal sector. Developed using an Agile methodology, the platform's architecture leverages modern full-stack web technologies including Next.js (React), Supabase (PostgreSQL), and Prisma ORM. To solve the trust deficit, the system implements a secure financial state machine (escrow) that holds client funds during the service lifecycle and releases them upon verified completion. Furthermore, the platform integrates an AI-powered hybrid search engine to interpret natural language consumer queries, an automated identity verification workflow utilizing the Ghana Card infrastructure, and an AI-assisted dispute resolution framework for objective administrative mediation.

The implementation of ArtisanConnect demonstrates the technical feasibility of secure digital intermediation in resource-constrained environments. By explicitly embedding institutional trust mechanisms and advanced Artificial Intelligence into the platform architecture, this project provides a scalable software blueprint for formalizing the artisanal sector. Ultimately, the study contributes to Information and Communication Technologies for Development (ICT4D) by proving that trust engineering and cutting-edge AI can effectively mitigate financial risks, reduce search friction, and foster equitable economic participation among blue-collar workers in Sub-Saharan Africa.
